Connecticut Disabled Veteran Benefits (2026)

State benefits to look into, each with its official source. This page is not a rating filter. Federal benefits are in the checker.

Where to get help

The Connecticut Department of Veterans Affairs Office of Advocacy and Assistance has accredited Veteran Service Officers in district offices who help with federal, state and local benefits (DVA district offices).

Official source: DVA district offices · Last verified 2026-11-03

Rating guide

100% permanent and total (P&T): no property tax on your home, or on one car if you don't own a home (item 1) · 10%+: disabled veteran exemption (item 2) · Connecticut residents with any service-connected disability: free lifetime state park pass (item 7) · Wartime service plus a qualifying disability: no-fee DV plates (item 4) · Surviving spouses: item 3 · Military retirees: retired pay not taxed (item 5) · Wartime veterans: tuition waiver (item 8). (page 1 rating guide; each part matches the item it points to)

Last verified 2026-11-03. Official sources are on each benefit below.

Connecticut state benefits

Last verified 2026-11-03

  • No Property Tax at 100% P&T

    • Connecticut resident with a VA 100% permanent and total service-connected rating
    • Your share of the home you own and live in is fully exempt; if you don't own a home, one motor vehicle garaged in Connecticut is
    • Towns may cap it at the town's median home assessment. You can't also take the item 2 exemption
    • Apply to your town assessor every year by January 1 (DD214 recorded with your town clerk by September 30); late filing is allowed up to a year after you get your rating: Legislative research report (PDF)
    What to gather (3)
    • VA letter showing 100% P&T
    • DD214 recorded with your town clerk
    • Town application (an OPM form)

    Official source: Legislative research report (PDF) · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • Disabled Veteran Property Tax Exemption (10%+)

    • Connecticut resident with a VA rating of at least 10%, or federal compensation for loss of an arm or leg
    • A basic exemption of $2,000 to $3,500 of assessed value by rating (or age 65+), plus an income-based amount; severe disabilities add $5,000 or $10,000
    • At 100%: at least $10,500 if income is $18,000 or less ($21,000 married), otherwise at least $5,250. Many towns give more
    • File your DD214 with the town clerk and your proof with the assessor: Mandatory relief report (PDF) · amounts by town
    What to gather (3)
    • VA letter showing your rating
    • DD214 recorded with your town clerk
    • Income information if claiming the higher amount

    Official source: amounts by town · Official source: Mandatory relief report (PDF) · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • Surviving Spouse and Minor Child Exemptions

    • Unremarried surviving spouse (or minor child) of a veteran who qualified for item 1 or item 2, while they live in Connecticut
    • Keeps the exemption to the same extent the veteran had, or would have had
    • Spouse or child of a service member who died on active duty from service: a $3,000 basic exemption plus an income-based amount
    • Apply with your town assessor: Mandatory relief report (PDF) · 100% P&T report
    What to gather (3)
    • Two affidavits from people with no stake in the claim showing the veteran's service, discharge or death in service, and your relationship to the veteran (ask your assessor what else to bring)
    • Veteran's VA rating letter
    • Veteran's DD214

    Official source: 100% P&T report · Official source: Mandatory relief report (PDF) · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • No-Fee Disabled Veteran Plates

    • Connecticut resident veteran who served in wartime and has a VA-certified service-connected amputation or loss of use of all or part of an arm or leg, blindness, paraplegia, hemiplegia or traumatic brain injury
    • Up to 3 registrations a year with no registration fee, plus an overtime parking card; title and other fees still apply
    • An unremarried surviving spouse may keep the plate for life at no fee
    • First request in person at DMV Wethersfield; renewals at any full-service DMV: DMV class 14 sheet (PDF) · DVA plates page
    What to gather (2)
    • VA letter on VA letterhead approving Disabled Veteran plates
    • Proof you own or lease the vehicle

    Official source: DMV class 14 sheet (PDF) · Official source: DVA plates page · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • Military Retired Pay Not Taxed

    • Retired members of the armed forces or National Guard, and survivor benefit recipients
    • Military retired pay is fully exempt from Connecticut income tax
    • Payments to a former spouse under a divorce decree don't qualify
    • Subtract it when you file: CT-1040 instructions (PDF)
    What to gather (2)
    • Your DFAS 1099-R
    • Form CT-1040, Schedule 1, line 44

    Official source: CT-1040 instructions (PDF) · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • VA Disability Pay Not Taxed

    • Disability pensions and other benefits paid for relief of injuries or to disabled veterans, and other benefits paid to a veteran or beneficiary under the laws relating to veterans, are treated the same for Connecticut income tax as for federal income tax
    • If those amounts are excluded from federal gross income, they are not subject to Connecticut income tax

    Official source: DRS IP 2019(5) · Last verified 2026-11-09

  • Free Lifetime Disabled Veteran Park Pass

    • Connecticut resident with a service-connected disability (any rating)
    • Free lifetime pass, including admission at Gillette Castle, Dinosaur and Fort Trumbull State Parks
    • Only for you (not transferable); not valid for camping or special events. CT-plated cars already park free
    • Get it at a DEEP district office or by mail to the State Parks Division (860-424-3200): DEEP special passes
    What to gather (2)
    • Copy of your CT driver's license (or other proof of residency)
    • VA card or VA benefit letter showing a service-connected disability

    Official source: DEEP special passes · Last verified 2026-11-03

  • Tuition Waiver at Public Colleges

    • A wartime veteran, generally with at least 90 days of service during a specified war period, or separated earlier because of a service-connected VA-rated disability or a line-of-duty injury
    • Accepted at a Connecticut public college and living in Connecticut when accepted
    • The college waives tuition that exceeds amounts covered by the Post-9/11 GI Bill, for credit-bearing undergraduate and graduate programs at the community-technical colleges, the Connecticut State University System, and UConn
    • The waiver does not cover other charges or fees, such as student activity fees and room and board

    Official source: OLR report 2022-R-0186 (CGS §§ 10a-77, 10a-99, 10a-105) · Last verified 2026-11-09
